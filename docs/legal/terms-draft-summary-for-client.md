# FleetGrid Terms: summary for Annie

**This is a draft for the lawyer to review. It is not legal advice, and nothing is published yet.**

## What the Terms do

The Terms are the rules for using FleetGrid. They say FleetGrid is a technology platform. Carriers post shifts and drivers book them. FleetGrid does not hire drivers, run loads or decide who is qualified. Carriers do that. The Terms also say who is responsible when something goes wrong, what carriers pay and how disputes are handled.

## The 5 biggest changes, and why

1. **Carriers pay a fee to hire a driver directly. They are not banned from it.** A ban on hiring can create legal risk. A clear price is easier to defend and fairer. Drivers stay free to work for anyone.
2. **Carriers carry the risk, in plain view.** Carriers agree to protect FleetGrid from claims, even if FleetGrid is partly at fault. They also promise to follow trucking rules and carry insurance. For this to hold up in Texas, the wording has to be very clear and easy to see.
3. **Labels say only what is true.** "Roster-Ready" means documents are on file and were checked for completeness. It does not mean a driver is safe or qualified. This protects FleetGrid if a carrier relies on a label.
4. **No-shows become a fair booking rule.** After repeated no-shows, a driver's new bookings pause for a set time. The driver gets notice and can dispute it. This keeps FleetGrid from looking like a driver's boss.
5. **Clear fees and a clear trial.** The Terms define when a shift counts as filled. Before a carrier gives a card, the trial screen shows the first charge and its date. Cancelling is as easy as signing up.

The Terms also move from Virginia law to Texas law. Current users will need to accept the new Terms.

## Decisions we need from you

- **Hiring fee:** how much, how it drops as a carrier books more shifts, and for how long it applies.
- **What carriers see:** driver profiles, or only counts of available drivers.
- **No-show pause:** how many days, and how a driver gets back on.
- **Medical cards:** how long we keep them.
- **Labels:** the final wording.
- **Driver disputes:** Texas courts (Option A) or arbitration, a private process outside court (Option B). The lawyer will advise.
- **Marketing texts:** will we ever send them? The Terms say no. Changing that later needs new consent from drivers.
