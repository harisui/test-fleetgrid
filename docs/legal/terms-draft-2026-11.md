## Booking Rules

- Shift offers go by text to drivers whose profile fits the shift and who have agreed to shift texts. Sending an offer does not promise work.
- A driver books a shift by replying YES with the shift's code. Bookings are confirmed in the order replies are received. A driver whose reply arrives after the shift is booked is told so by text.
- A driver cannot hold two bookings that overlap or that have less than 90 minutes between them. A reply that would create one is refused, and the driver is told by text.
- If a driver does not show up, the carrier releases the shift in FleetGrid so it can be offered again, and the no-show is recorded.
- **Booking reliability.** A driver recorded as a no-show for [two] booked shifts within 90 days will have new bookings paused for [X] days. The driver gets notice, may dispute a no-show record and may ask for reinstatement at any time. A pause doesn't affect working for any carrier off the platform.

## Questions for your review

1. **Indemnity (Carriers).** Does the wording meet the Texas fair-notice rules? Does the choice of Texas law matter for a Virginia LLC with Houston operations? Should carriers tick a separate acknowledgment checkbox for the indemnity at signup? (Not built; see "Engineering gaps".)
2. **Direct Engagement Fee.** It is now a priced option, not a ban. Is the wording sound as a price rather than a penalty? Could a fee for hiring a driver weaken "not a staffing agency"? Could it raise no-poach or antitrust concerns, even though it binds carriers only? A carrier that finds a driver through a profile and hires them without ever booking a shift is not covered. Is that trade-off acceptable?
3. **Labels ("No Verification by FleetGrid").** Do "Roster-Ready" and "Documented profile" create any implied representation or duty of care, given that FleetGrid reviews uploads and keeps documents? Could "Cleared for [Carrier]" create a representation by FleetGrid, or a risk if a carrier's statement is wrong?
4. **Consumer reports.** Driver cards show self-reported driving-record and credential answers to subscribing carriers. Does this raise any Fair Credit Reporting Act question?
5. **Worker classification.** Do the "Booking Rules" (the booking-reliability pause, offers sent by FleetGrid) sit comfortably with "does not supervise", under federal and Texas tests?
6. **Medical card.** Carriers view a medical card for a booked shift, up to 7 days after it ends. What retention period and what consent wording should apply, here and in the Privacy Policy?
7. **Disputes.** Is the two-track approach right? Which driver option? Does anything change for the Virginia transition?
8. **Fees and Trial.** A flat fee per completed fill ties FleetGrid's fee to a driver working a shift. Does that weaken "technology platform only" or "not a staffing agency", or affect broker status? What must the auto-renewal disclosure say for a card-on-file trial?
9. **Planned driver consent forms.** An FCRA authorization for MVRs ordered by carriers, and a Clearinghouse limited-query consent. See the FCRA research note: carriers keep these consents, and FleetGrid may host carrier-branded forms only. Is that right?
10. **Driver eligibility.** Is the age wording right for interstate and Texas intrastate operation?
